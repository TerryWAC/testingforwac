    How much deposit do I actually need?

    5% is the practical minimum for most residential mortgages, and there are lenders who work at that level. But the rate improves at each band — 10%, 15%, and again at 25% — so if you're close to a threshold it's often worth waiting a few months.

    Buy-to-let is different: expect to need at least 25%.

    I'm self-employed. Is that a problem?

    No, but it changes which lenders to approach. Some want two or three years of accounts; others will consider one. Some use your net profit, others your salary plus dividends, and for contractors a few will work from your day rate.

    The difference between the most and least generous lender on the same set of accounts can be substantial — which is the whole reason to have someone search properly.

    My credit isn't perfect. Should I bother?

    Usually yes. A missed phone bill three years ago is not the obstacle people fear. Defaults, CCJs and recent arrears are more serious, but specialist lenders price for them rather than refusing outright.

    Get a copy of your credit file before we speak — it makes the first conversation far more useful. And avoid making multiple applications yourself; each search leaves a mark.

    When should I start looking at a remortgage?

    About six months before your current deal ends. Most offers are valid for three to six months, so you can lock in a rate early and still move to a better one if rates fall. Leave it too late and you roll onto the lender's standard variable rate, which is almost always the most expensive option available to you.

    How long does the whole thing take?

    A decision in principle usually takes 24 hours. From full application to formal offer is typically two to four weeks, depending on the lender and how quickly the valuation happens. Purchases then depend on the conveyancing chain — that part is outside anyone's control, but I'll keep the lender side moving.
